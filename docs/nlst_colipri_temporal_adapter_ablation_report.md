# COLIPRI Temporal Latent Adapter Ablation

> **Evaluation invalidated by code audit:** the original downstream results below used shuffled training exports with labels in manifest order. Their near-chance AUROCs cannot establish prognostic information loss. Corrected evaluation is running from the existing checkpoints in `outputs/colipri_temporal_adapter_corrected/`, with verified row alignment, native and adapted current/future controls, and matched mini-batch probes. All 30 checkpoints have `lambda_sigreg=0`. The historical tables below are retained for provenance, not scientific interpretation.

## Executive summary

This experiment tested compact JEPA/LeWorldModel-inspired future-state predictors on frozen 864-d COLIPRI embeddings. The COLIPRI encoder remained frozen. The experiment reused the existing 1,984 longitudinal pairs and patient-level split, and trained seven configurations:

- identity predictor;
- direct linear predictor in the frozen 864-d space;
- raw residual MLP in the frozen 864-d space;
- temporal adapter plus direct prediction;
- temporal adapter plus residual prediction;
- temporal adapter plus residual prediction and time;
- temporal adapter plus residual prediction, velocity, and time.

The main result is negative but informative: the adapter variants reconstruct their own 128-d learned latent states extremely well, but those states lose most of the prognostic information present in the original COLIPRI representation. The raw-space predictors also do not improve over the previous pooled MLP baseline.

The adapter therefore optimized predictability of a learned coordinate system rather than preservation of clinically useful information.

## Experimental setup

- Frozen COLIPRI input: 864 dimensions.
- Temporal adapter: `864 → Linear(256) → LayerNorm → GELU → Linear(128)`.
- Residual predictors used two small MLP blocks with dropout 0.1.
- Optional normalized time input: `Δt / 365.25`.
- Optional velocity: `z_t - z_{t-1}` when the previous scan was available.
- Future-state loss: MSE by default.
- SIGReg was disabled.
- Five world-model seeds were used for trainable models.
- Five downstream probe seeds were used.
- Downstream probes were trained separately after future-state training.
- All splits were patient-level and reused the existing cohort split.
- Downstream probes used the same post-t+1 cancer endpoints as previous experiments.

The GPU-native downstream evaluator completed 1,860 probe fits and logged a W&B summary run.

Test-set metrics are averaged over world-model seeds where applicable.

| Model | MSE ↓ | Cosine ↑ | L2 ↓ | R² |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Identity | 0.000965 | 0.9753 | 0.856 | 0.560 |
| Linear direct | 0.000889 | 0.9768 | 0.837 | 0.595 |
| Raw residual MLP | 0.000886 | 0.9770 | 0.831 | 0.596 |
| Adapter direct | 0.000034 | 0.9979 | 0.065 | -1.833 |
| Adapter residual | 0.000002 | 1.0000 | 0.017 | 0.303 |
| Adapter + time | 0.000003 | 1.0000 | 0.017 | 0.294 |
| Adapter + velocity + time | 0.000003 | 1.0000 | 0.018 | 0.282 |

The raw-space models provide only a small reconstruction improvement over identity and are effectively equivalent to the previous raw/standardized-delta baseline in latent forecasting.

The adapter models report much smaller errors, but those errors are measured in a different 128-d learned space. They should not be compared numerically with the 864-d COLIPRI metrics without an adapter-space identity baseline and a raw-space decoded evaluation.

## Prognostic results

The following table reports AUROC for the predicted future representation. Results are averaged over probe seeds and world-model seeds. The identity row corresponds to the current frozen COLIPRI representation.

| Model | Cancer 1y | Cancer 2y | Cancer 3y |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Identity/current `z_t` | 0.713 | 0.649 | 0.636 |
| Linear direct | 0.558 | 0.523 | 0.522 |
| Raw residual MLP | 0.532 | 0.512 | 0.524 |
| Adapter direct | 0.508 | 0.502 | 0.503 |
| Adapter residual | 0.463 | 0.474 | 0.480 |
| Adapter + time | 0.491 | 0.498 | 0.503 |
| Adapter + velocity + time | 0.500 | 0.511 | 0.513 |

The previous pooled MLP baseline reached approximately 0.717, 0.667, and 0.654 AUROC at 1y, 2y, and 3y. Thus, none of the new predictors improves prognosis. The current representation remains more informative than every predicted future representation in this experiment.

The velocity-plus-time adapter shows a higher AUROC for the predicted change at 1y, approximately 0.632, but this does not transfer to the predicted future state, whose AUROC is approximately 0.500. This isolated delta result should therefore be treated as exploratory and unstable rather than as evidence of a useful future-state model.

## Why does the adapter reconstruct well but lose prognosis?

### 1. The target is generated by the same learnable adapter

For adapter models, the target is not the original future COLIPRI embedding. It is:

```text
z_t1 = adapter(h_t1)
```

and the predictor is trained to reproduce that learned output. The adapter is optimized jointly with the predictor, so it can make the future prediction problem easier by removing variation that is difficult to forecast. That variation may nevertheless contain the strongest cancer or diagnostic signal.

This creates a predictability-information tradeoff:

```text
easy-to-predict z  ≠  clinically informative z
```

### 2. MSE rewards predictable average structure, not rare clinical events

The training objective sees only future representations. It does not know that a small subset of focal abnormalities or cancer-related changes are important. The easiest way to reduce MSE is to retain stable, common population structure and suppress unpredictable directions.

Prognostic information is often concentrated in exactly those low-frequency directions. Removing them can dramatically improve MSE while degrading AUROC.

### 3. The adapter can contract or collapse clinically relevant variation

The adapter has only 128 dimensions and no information-preservation constraint. LayerNorm and the bottleneck make it easy to produce a compact, smooth state. The residual predictor can then achieve near-perfect cosine similarity in that compressed space.

Near-perfect cosine similarity therefore means that the predictor matches the adapter's geometry; it does not mean that the geometry still contains the original COLIPRI signal.

### 4. There is no reconstruction or invertibility pressure back to COLIPRI space

The adapter is not required to preserve information needed to recover `h_t` or `h_t1`. There is no decoder, whitening constraint, variance floor, covariance matching, or frozen-feature reconstruction loss. As a result, information can be discarded permanently at the 864→128 projection.

### 5. Prognosis is evaluated in the learned adapter space, not in native COLIPRI space

The downstream classifier receives `z_t` or `z_hat_t1`, not the original 864-d COLIPRI vector. This makes the result a test of the adapter representation as a clinical representation, not only a test of the transition predictor.

That is a valid scientific question, but it conflates two effects:

1. whether the adapter preserves useful COLIPRI information;
2. whether the predictor forecasts future information.

The present results indicate that the first effect is already failing.

### 6. The identity comparison was not fully matched in adapter space

The current identity row uses the original frozen 864-d representation. A fair adapter-space identity baseline should be:

```text
z_hat_t1 = z_t = adapter(h_t)
```

with the same adapter frozen after training, evaluated against `z_t1 = adapter(h_t1)`. This would separate transition-prediction performance from the information loss caused by the adapter itself.

This caveat does not rescue the current downstream result: the adapter representations are close to chance for prognosis, while the native COLIPRI representation is substantially predictive. It does mean that future adapter experiments need a stricter set of controls.

## Main conclusions

1. The new raw-space linear and residual predictors do not improve over the previous pooled MLP.
2. Adapter variants achieve excellent reconstruction in their own 128-d space.
3. That excellent reconstruction is misleading as a clinical criterion because adapter representations lose prognostic information.
4. Time conditioning and velocity do not recover the lost information.
5. The bottleneck is not simply transition-model capacity. The representation objective itself is misaligned with information preservation.

## Recommended next experiments

### A. Add an adapter-space identity baseline

Train the adapter first or jointly, then evaluate:

```text
adapter(h_t) → adapter(h_t1)
```

without a learned transition model. This quantifies information loss due solely to the adapter.

### B. Preserve native COLIPRI information explicitly

Add an auxiliary reconstruction term:

```text
L = L_future(z_hat_t1, z_t1)
  + λ_recon ||decoder(z_t1) - h_t1||²
```

Alternatively, use a wider latent dimension such as 256 or 512 and compare native-space prognosis.

### C. Use a frozen adapter for the first transition comparison

Fit the adapter only to preserve current/future COLIPRI information, freeze it, and then train the temporal predictor. This avoids the adapter changing its coordinate system to make the transition easier.

### D. Add information-preserving variance/covariance constraints

Use variance floors and covariance matching, or a VICReg-style objective, on adapter outputs. SIGReg alone may improve distributional shape without guaranteeing preservation of clinical directions.

### E. Evaluate prediction in native 864-d space

Even if the predictor operates in a compact latent, decode its prediction back to 864 dimensions and evaluate:

- native-space MSE;
- native-space cosine;
- native-space prognosis;
- native-space diagnostic recovery.

### F. Do not select models by reconstruction alone

Use validation criteria that include both future-state loss and preservation of current-state decodability. Prognostic labels should remain excluded from world-model training, but frozen diagnostic probes can be used as a representation-quality audit.
